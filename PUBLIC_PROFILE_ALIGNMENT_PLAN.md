**Platforms:** hugomartins.eu · LinkedIn · GitHub

## 1. Executive decision

The public profile should no longer lead with the broad category **Human-Centred AI for Learning and Work**. That remains the intellectual umbrella, but it is too wide to function as the primary market position.

The new front-door position should be:

> **AI Literacy Educator for Higher Education**

The supporting promise should be:

> I design AI-literacy curriculum, assessment and hands-on learning that prepares university students to use generative and agentic AI with knowledge, judgement and accountability.

The immediate proof should be:

> Lecturer in CC0007 Science and Technology for Humanity at Nanyang Technological University · 42 tutorial classes · 1,700+ undergraduates · four consecutive semesters · applied AI teaching tools and curriculum design.

This positioning is specific enough for hiring managers to remember, internationally portable across the target markets, and supported by unusually concrete implementation evidence.

### Expert-positioning rule

The profile should create an expert impression without repeatedly calling Hugo an “expert.” Use the professional formulation **“AI literacy educator specialising in higher education curriculum and assessment”** in biographies. Reserve **“expert”** for third-party descriptions, search metadata where appropriate, event introductions or roles whose official title uses the term.

Expertise should be demonstrated through:

- teaching at institution-wide undergraduate scale;
- a clear and original intellectual framework;
- detailed curriculum and assessment case studies;
- documented teaching outcomes and recognised credentials;
- conference participation and substantial public writing;
- inspectable applied tools with transparent limitations;
- careful separation of personal contribution from institutional strategy.

The tone should be precise, measured and evidence-led. Avoid “thought leader,” “AI visionary,” “guru,” inflated transformation claims and generic enthusiasm about the future of AI.

### Role of the broader themes

- **AI literacy in higher education** becomes the primary identity.
- **Adaptive expertise and the future of work** becomes the distinctive intellectual thesis.
- **Human-centred AI** becomes the design philosophy.
- **Organisational behaviour** becomes the analytical foundation.
- **Applied AI systems** become evidence of hands-on capability.
- **Service systems and organisational AI adoption** remain secondary extensions, not homepage competitors.

1. Directors of centres for teaching, learning, digital education and educational technology.

3. Universities hiring lecturers in AI literacy, AI and education, digital competency, educational technology or interdisciplinary core curricula.
4. Learning-innovation and academic-development teams seeking curriculum, assessment or faculty-capability specialists.
5. National academies, government-linked capability programmes and executive-education providers building responsible AI capability.
6. Conference organisers, policy groups and industry–academic initiatives seeking speakers on AI readiness, adaptive expertise and higher education.

Position Hugo as an educator who can contribute to these goals through curriculum, assessment and educator capability. #### United Arab Emirates — applied AI learning and institutional readiness

Do not use innovation language without showing the programme, framework, artifact or result behind it.

#### Europe — rigorous AI literacy, assessment and responsible adoption

Lead with:

European positioning should acknowledge that “Europe” is not one hiring market. Prioritise international universities, English-medium programmes, business schools, teaching-and-learning centres and EU-facing education initiatives where the working language and role requirements match Hugo’s verified profile. The [European Commission’s AI literacy guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/ai-talent-skills-and-literacy) makes AI literacy a concrete organisational concern under Article 4 of the AI Act, but the profile must not imply legal or compliance expertise. The credible claim is expertise in designing and teaching AI literacy, with responsible-use principles that are relevant to the European context.

Build the European shortlist in two stages:

1. Verify citizenship/work rights, language proficiency, mobility and minimum role conditions.
2. Prioritise English-medium institutions and roles in the UK, Ireland, the Netherlands, the Nordics and international universities elsewhere in Europe; add Portugal or other language-specific markets only where proficiency and role fit are confirmed.

Keep EU AI Act language for EU-facing opportunities. Do not apply it generically to UK, Swiss or other non-EU roles.

Create three application packs:

2. **Saudi/UAE pack:** curriculum transformation, capacity building, applied AI, interdisciplinary learning and future-ready graduates.

## 8. Teaching philosophy framework

The teaching philosophy should be distilled into a memorable five-part model used across the website, LinkedIn and talks.

### 1. Deep knowledge before supervision

Students cannot reliably evaluate confident AI output in a field they do not understand. AI literacy must reinforce substantive knowledge rather than replace it.

### 2. Productive friction before effortless output

Learning requires attempts, correction, revision and reflection. Educational design should preserve the effort that builds capability even when AI can produce an answer more efficiently.

### 3. Framing before solving

When plausible solutions are cheap, the scarce capability is defining the right problem, identifying stakeholders and recognising what the system leaves out.

### 4. Observed performance before unverified artefacts

Assessment should include oral defence, live problem solving, accountable project work and visible reasoning—not only unsupervised written products.

### 5. Apprenticeship before automation

If entry-level production no longer develops expertise automatically, universities must deliberately create authentic practice, feedback, peer learning and increasing responsibility.

Working signature line:

> AI can make performance easier. Education must still make expertise possible.
